Research

Rivers in semi-arid regions swing between floods and drying. We study how those swings, and the ways climate change and water use are shifting them, structure freshwater communities and the ecosystem functions rivers provide.

Flow regimes and environmental flows

We characterize flow regimes from long-term gauge records and quantify how they have been altered. We link flow to habitat through integrated hydrological, hydraulic and habitat models, and build flow–ecology relationships for fish and macroinvertebrates to inform environmental flow standards.

  • Indicators of hydrologic alteration and flow-regime classification
  • Hydrological–hydraulic–habitat modelling
  • Flow–ecology relationships under present and future climates

Intermittent rivers and connectivity

Many Texas streams stop flowing for part of the year. We measure when and where drying happens, how it fragments river networks, and how communities persist and recover through drying and rewetting.

  • Drying–rewetting dynamics and flow intermittency metrics
  • Network connectivity, including graph-based approaches
  • Community resistance and recovery after drying

Environmental DNA and biodiversity

Environmental DNA lets us survey biodiversity across many sites and visits without capturing organisms. We pair eDNA and diatom metabarcoding with hydrological monitoring to see how communities track changes in flow.

  • Water eDNA and benthic diatom metabarcoding
  • Biodiversity along flowing, drying and rewetting reaches

Stream ecosystem function

Biodiversity is only part of the picture. We also measure what streams do: how much carbon they fix and respire, how quickly they take up nutrients, and how much carbon dioxide and methane they release. We ask how these functions shift as flow regimes change and as reaches dry and rewet.

  • Stream metabolism (gross primary production and ecosystem respiration)
  • Nutrient uptake and retention
  • Greenhouse gas fluxes across flowing and drying phases

Watersheds under climate and land-use change

We use watershed models such as SWAT+ and statistical analysis of long records to assess how climate and land-use change alter water availability, extreme events, low flows and drought.

  • Watershed modelling and climate-impact assessment
  • Trends in precipitation, temperature and streamflow extremes
  • Soil, groundwater and surface-water interactions

Where we work

Our field research centers on the rivers of the Texas Hill Country and Edwards Plateau, where spring-fed reaches, flash floods and seasonal drying sit side by side. Earlier work took the group to lowland rivers in Poland, river basins in southern India, and watersheds in Kentucky and North Carolina.

  • TexasHill Country and Edwards Plateau rivers
  • EuropeLowland and floodplain rivers in Poland
  • AsiaCauvery and Godavari basins, India
  • US SoutheastKentucky River and Haw River basins

Collaborations

We work with colleagues at Texas State University and with research groups across the United States and Europe, including long-running collaborations on environmental flows and catchment modelling with Warsaw University of Life Sciences, Poland.
