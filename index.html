How rivers flow, dry and recover, and what that means for the life in them.

We are a hydroecology lab at Texas State University. We combine stream gauges, watershed models, field surveys and environmental DNA to understand how changing flow regimes shape freshwater biodiversity and ecosystem function, starting with the rivers of the Texas Hill Country.

What we work on

Connected questions about how water moves through river networks, the life it supports and the work rivers do.

Flow regimes and environmental flows

How altered flows affect fish and macroinvertebrates, and how much water rivers need to keep functioning.

Intermittent rivers

When and where streams stop flowing, and how river networks and their communities respond to drying and rewetting.

Biodiversity and stream function

How freshwater biodiversity and stream ecosystem functions respond as flow regimes change.

Watersheds under change

SWAT+ modelling and long-term records to assess climate and land-use effects on floods, droughts and low flows.
